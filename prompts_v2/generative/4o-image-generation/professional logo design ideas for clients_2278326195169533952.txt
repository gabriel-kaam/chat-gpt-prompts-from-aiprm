Professional Logo Design Ideas for Clients
[TARGETLANGUAGE]

Client brief:
[PROMPT]

Based on the brief, create a response using the following structure:

## 1. Strategic Introduction
Start with a short, thoughtful paragraph explaining the best overall logo direction for this brand. Connect the logo idea to the client's business, target audience, industry, brand personality, and core message. Explain what the logo should communicate and how it should stand apart from generic industry logos.

## 2. Three Promising Logo Concepts
Develop three distinct logo concepts. Give each concept a creative name and include:

- **Symbol:** Describe the icon, shape, monogram, or visual element.
- **Hidden Meaning:** Explain the symbolism and how it connects to the business or brand story.
- **Style:** Describe the visual direction, such as minimal, geometric, premium, corporate, bold, futuristic, or elegant.
- **Typography:** Recommend the appropriate typeface style and wordmark treatment.
- **Best For:** Explain the strengths and ideal positioning of this concept.

Clearly identify your top recommendation and explain why it is the strongest option for this specific client.

Avoid generic concepts. Every idea should have a meaningful connection to the client's brief.

## 3. Recommended Design Direction
Develop the strongest concept into a more specific visual direction.

Include:
- Logo icon and wordmark arrangement.
- Icon construction and visual details.
- Recommended color palette with color names and HEX codes.
- Primary and secondary typography.
- Suggested tagline or supporting text, if appropriate.

If useful, include a simple conceptual logo mockup or visual reference. Clearly distinguish a conceptual direction from a finished, production-ready logo.

## 4. Typography and Composition
Explain how the icon, brand name, and supporting text should work together.

Discuss:
- Wordmark weight, proportions, and letter spacing.
- Icon-to-wordmark balance.
- Horizontal and stacked logo variations.
- Scalability and legibility.
- Suitability for monochrome, light, and dark backgrounds.

## 5. What to Avoid
List 3–5 specific design mistakes that could weaken the brand identity. Explain what to avoid based on the client's industry, requirements, and positioning.

## 6. Core Brand Concept
Finish with a memorable one-line brand idea or tagline that summarizes the visual identity's central message. Add a short explanation of how the logo communicates that idea.

End with a subtle, practical next step, such as developing three visual sketches, comparing icon directions, or creating a refined logo presentation.

### Writing Style
- Write in clear, professional English.
- Use structured headings, concise paragraphs, and bullet points.
- Be creative but practical; explain the reasoning behind each design decision.
- Avoid unnecessary branding jargon and vague descriptions.
- Prioritize originality, simplicity, memorability, and real-world usability.
- Respect all explicit client requirements, including colors, symbols, typography, references, and restrictions.
- Do not invent requirements or claim that a logo is unique or trademark-safe without verification.
- Make the response feel like a thoughtful creative director presenting ideas to a designer, not a generic AI-generated list.

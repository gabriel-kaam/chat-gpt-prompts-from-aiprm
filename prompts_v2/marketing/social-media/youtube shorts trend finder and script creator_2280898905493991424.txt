# YOUTUBE SHORTS TREND & PRODUCTION ENGINE

INPUT TOPIC: [PROMPT]
OUTPUT LANGUAGE: [TARGETLANGUAGE]

Act as a professional YouTube trend researcher, Shorts strategist, title writer, thumbnail creative director and short-form video producer.

Transform the user's topic into a complete 60-second YouTube Shorts production package.

The user only needs to enter ONE topic. Do not request additional information.

All audience-facing text, titles, explanations, scripts and voice-overs must be written in [TARGETLANGUAGE]. Video-generation prompts may be written in English for compatibility with AI video tools.

## PHASE 1 — TREND DISCOVERY

Investigate promising YouTube content opportunities related to the topic.

When browsing or research tools are available:
- Prioritize recent, relevant YouTube videos and searches.
- Investigate emerging discussions and audience interests.
- Identify recurring successful content angles.
- Include reliable source links and dates.
- Distinguish measured information from editorial judgment.

When live research is unavailable:
- Explicitly state that current YouTube trends could not be verified.
- Identify 5 plausible content opportunities.
- Label them as content hypotheses, not confirmed trends.
- Never invent views, search volumes, sources or statistics.

OUTPUT:
A table with 5 content opportunities, a short explanation and an editorial opportunity score from 0–100.

The score is a qualitative estimate, not a measured probability of going viral.

Select the strongest opportunity.

## PHASE 2 — YOUTUBE TITLE ENGINE

Generate 5 compelling titles for the selected topic.

Use:
1. Curiosity gap
2. Surprising insight
3. Practical benefit
4. Unexpected contrast
5. Strong question

Requirements:
- Clear and mobile-friendly
- Preferably 35–60 characters
- Strong curiosity without misleading clickbait
- Relevant to the actual content

Select one final title and briefly explain the choice.

## PHASE 3 — THUMBNAIL CREATIVE DIRECTOR

Create one thumbnail concept.

Include:
- Main visual subject
- Emotional impact
- Composition
- Lighting and contrast
- Maximum 4 words of thumbnail text
- A complete English AI image-generation prompt

Create a vertical 9:16 cover concept. Keep the main subject centered and recognizable when cropped.

## PHASE 4 — SHORTS STORY ENGINE

Produce exactly 6 scenes.

FORMAT RULES:
- Vertical 9:16
- Total runtime: 60 seconds
- Exactly 6 scenes
- Each scene: exactly 10 seconds
- Maximum 14 spoken words per scene
- Maximum 84 spoken words overall
- All spoken words must be counted
- No additional spoken dialogue

STORY STRUCTURE:

Scene 1 (00–10 sec): HOOK
Introduce an immediate curiosity gap.

Scene 2 (10–20 sec): PROBLEM
Introduce a question, tension or surprising problem.

Scene 3 (20–30 sec): DISCOVERY
Reveal the first important insight.

Scene 4 (30–40 sec): DEEPER INSIGHT
Provide a meaningful example or unexpected development.

Scene 5 (40–50 sec): PAYOFF
Deliver the key answer or most valuable insight.

Scene 6 (50–60 sec): FINALE
Finish with a memorable statement and a natural invitation to engage.

FOR EVERY SCENE PROVIDE:

1. Scene number and precise timestamp.
2. Spoken voice-over in [TARGETLANGUAGE].
3. Exact spoken word count.
4. Visual description.
5. Detailed English AI video prompt specifying:
   - 9:16 vertical video
   - Exactly 10 seconds
   - Main subject
   - Scene environment
   - Camera movement
   - Lighting
   - Visual mood
   - Subject movement
   - Cinematic composition
6. Short on-screen text, maximum 5 words.
7. Recommended sound design.
8. Editing transition.

Each scene must connect naturally to the next.

Use concise narration, pauses and visual beats to fit the allocated time. Actual speech duration must be checked during audio production.

## PHASE 5 — COMPLETE VOICE-OVER

Combine the six voice-over segments into one clean narration script.

Do not modify or add spoken words.

Include natural pauses and voice-direction notes separately.

## PHASE 6 — PUBLISHING PACKAGE

Generate:
- Final YouTube title
- Short video description
- 3 relevant hashtags
- 5 useful search keywords
- Suggested pinned comment



## PHASES 7 — ADAPTIVE DOUBLE QUALITY CONTROL

## CORE PRINCIPLE

Quality is not achieved by checking boxes alone. Quality requires verified decisions, root-cause correction, dependency awareness and measurable improvement where evidence permits.

Execute two complete quality audits and two targeted correction stages.

Never skip an audit. Never report an unperformed test as completed.

## STAGE 1 — INITIAL QUALITY AUDIT

Analyze the complete first production draft.

### A. Structural Verification
Verify all mandatory production requirements:
- Exactly 5 content opportunities.
- Exactly 5 title candidates.
- One selected title and thumbnail concept.
- Exactly 6 video scenes.
- Exactly 10 seconds allocated to each scene.
- Maximum 14 spoken words per scene.
- Maximum 84 spoken words overall.
- Six complete video-generation prompts.
- Identical scene narration and master voice-over.
- Complete publishing package.

### B. Content & Evidence Verification
Examine:
- Topic relevance.
- Source reliability.
- Trend freshness.
- Factual accuracy.
- Unsupported claims.
- Narrative coherence.
- Viewer relevance.
- Thumbnail and title consistency.
- Voice-over naturalness.
- Visual continuity.
- Clarity and production feasibility.

### C. Root-Cause Analysis
For every detected problem, identify:
1. What failed?
2. Where did it fail?
3. Why did it fail?
4. Which other components are affected?
5. What is the smallest reliable correction?

Distinguish:
- Verified facts.
- Assumptions.
- Uncertainties.
- Actual blockers.

### D. Quality Report 1
Publish a structured report containing:
- Check performed.
- PASS / FAIL / UNVERIFIED.
- Evidence or actual result.
- Root cause when applicable.
- Severity: Critical / Major / Minor.
- Required correction.

Summarize the exact number of passed, failed and unverified checks.

## STAGE 2 — TARGETED CORRECTION

Correct failures according to their severity and dependencies.

Use this procedure:

ROOT CAUSE → REPAIR → UPDATE DEPENDENCIES → VERIFY REPAIR.

Repair only what needs correction.

Do not rewrite valid material without a specific reason.

After modifying a scene:
- Recount its spoken words.
- Update the master narration.
- Check visual synchronization.
- Check scene transitions.
- Confirm the timeline remains correct.

After modifying a factual claim:
- Check its supporting evidence.
- Update related narration.
- Update any dependent title, visual or publishing description.

Document each important correction.

## STAGE 3 — SECOND INDEPENDENT QUALITY AUDIT

Audit the entire corrected production package again.

Do not simply repeat the previous conclusions.

Re-evaluate all mandatory requirements against the actual corrected content.

Compare:
- Original draft.
- Corrected draft.
- Previously detected failures.
- Newly introduced risks.
- Remaining limitations.

Determine whether each correction:
- Solved the original problem.
- Preserved valid content.
- Introduced a new problem.
- Improved the relevant quality dimension.
- Created unnecessary complexity.

### Quality Decision

ACCEPT:
The modification resolves a verified problem or produces a clearly supported improvement without unacceptable regression.

REJECT:
The modification weakens accuracy, coherence, production constraints or another important quality dimension.

NO CHANGE:
No sufficiently supported improvement exists.

Revert rejected modifications when the previous valid version was better.

### Quality Report 2

Publish:
- All verification results.
- Errors resolved.
- New errors detected.
- Remaining unresolved issues.
- Accepted modifications.
- Rejected modifications.
- Reason for the final assessment.

Do not invent quantitative improvement percentages.

## STAGE 4 — FINAL IMPROVEMENT & DELIVERY

Apply the smallest necessary final corrections based on the second audit.

Revalidate every requirement affected by these corrections.

Do not silently mark unverified requirements as passed.

If a critical error remains, classify the product as NOT READY.

If all mandatory checkable constraints pass but external research or actual audio timing remains unverified, state those limitations clearly.

Publish the final result in this order:

1. FINAL TREND ANALYSIS
2. FINAL VIDEO CONCEPT
3. FIVE TITLES AND SELECTED TITLE
4. FINAL THUMBNAIL CONCEPT
5. COMPLETE SIX-SCENE STORYBOARD
6. FINAL MASTER VOICE-OVER
7. PUBLISHING PACKAGE
8. QUALITY REPORT 1
9. CORRECTION LOG 1
10. QUALITY REPORT 2
11. FINAL IMPROVEMENT LOG
12. FINAL STATUS

## COMPLETION RULE

The workflow is complete only when:

- Two real quality audits have been performed.
- Both audit analyses are visible.
- Detected errors have been investigated.
- Correctable errors have been repaired.
- Affected dependencies have been updated.
- Corrections have been compared with the previous version.
- Remaining limitations have been disclosed.
- The best verified production package has been delivered.

Quality takes priority over superficial completion.

Do not claim perfection. Demonstrate the quality of the result.
